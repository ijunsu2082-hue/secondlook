For anyone waiting on a breast imaging result

Got a breast imaging result? Let's go through it together.

After a mammogram (an X-ray picture of the breast) or an ultrasound (a picture made with sound waves), a doctor writes a report. It is written for other doctors, so it can be hard to read. SecondLook explains it in plain words, answers your questions, and helps you get ready for your next visit. It does not replace your doctor.

Words you might see

Mammogram
An X-ray picture of the breast. It can show changes too small to feel.
Ultrasound
A picture made with sound waves. It can show whether a lump is solid or filled with fluid.
Biopsy
A test that takes a tiny piece of tissue from the breast, usually with a needle, so a doctor can look at it under a microscope.
Radiologist
A doctor who reads X-rays, ultrasounds and other pictures, and writes the report.
Pathologist
A doctor who studies tissue from a biopsy under a microscope to find out what it is.
BI-RADS
A score from 0 to 6 that radiologists use to sum up what they saw and what should happen next.
Benign
Not cancer.
Malignant
Cancer.
Breast density
How much of the breast is dense tissue (glands and fibers) instead of fat. Dense tissue looks white on a mammogram and can hide small lumps.
MRI
A scan that uses magnets and radio waves to make detailed pictures. It is used for some people at higher risk.
Lymph nodes
Small bean-shaped glands that are part of your immune system. Some are under the arm.
BRCA1 and BRCA2
Genes that help protect against cancer. When they have a harmful change, the chance of breast and ovarian cancer goes up.

SecondLook is a learning tool, not a medical device. It cannot tell you whether you have cancer. No doctor has reviewed it yet. Please do not use it to put off care from a doctor.

Before you chat: where your words go

Everything else on SecondLook stays in your browser. The chat is different. To write an answer, it has to send your words to an outside company.

  • What is sent: each question you type, and the full text of the report you explained above (if you did).
  • Where it goes: to our server, and then to OpenAI, the company that runs the AI.
  • What happens to it: our server does not save it. OpenAI may keep it for a short time under its own rules, which we do not control.
  • What to leave out: your name, birth date, address, and anything else that could point to you. If you just want to try it, use one of the made-up sample reports.

Screening planner

Screening means checking people who have no symptoms. A screening mammogram is a routine breast X-ray. BRCA1 and BRCA2 are genes that, when changed, raise the chance of breast and ovarian cancer.

Biopsy lab for learning

When a biopsy (a test that takes a tiny piece of tissue, usually with a needle) is done on a lump, a doctor called a pathologist looks at the cells. In a well known study from Wisconsin, software measured the center of each cell (the nucleus) in microscope images. Our small model reads those 30 measurements. Pick a real example it has not seen before and see which measurements push the answer toward benign (not cancer) or malignant (cancer).

How it works

This is not medical advice. SecondLook is a learning tool made for the Code the Cure hackathon. If you notice a change in your breast, contact a doctor. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Reports, images, symptoms and lab sliders are handled in your browser and are not sent anywhere. The one exception is the chat: your question and report text go to our server and to OpenAI to get an answer.
